# Units and vectors — references for AO1 + AO2

Journal entry: “a01+a02 (knowledge+problems). Multiple-choice questions: units, vectors.”

References checked on 9 September 2026. Working assumption: Cambridge IGCSE Physics
0625/0972, based on the project context; the journal entry alone does not identify the course.

## What to prepare

The wording likely refers to **AO1: Knowledge with understanding** and **AO2: Handling
information and problem-solving**. Prepare both definitions and questions that require
applying them. The entry identifies assessment objectives and topics; by itself it does
not establish which particular skills he got wrong.

For IGCSE, the [official 2026–2028 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697209-2026-2028-syllabus.pdf)
gives the assessment objectives on printed p.10, scalars/vectors in §1.1 on p.12, and the
required quantities, symbols, units and prefixes on pp.50–51. Scalars/vectors and the
resultant of two perpendicular forces or velocities are Supplement/Extended content.

Suggested focus:

- **Units:** quantity versus unit versus symbol; common units; prefixes and powers of ten;
  conversions involving length, area, volume and speed; keeping units in calculations.
- **Vectors:** magnitude and direction; scalar/vector classification; speed versus velocity;
  arrows drawn to scale; opposite directions; resultant forces and velocities, including
  right-angle cases if studying Extended.

## 2. A second free textbook for explanations and worked problems

**OpenStax, Physics**, by Paul Peter Urone and Roger Hinrichs, is a school-level textbook.

- [§1.3 Physical Quantities and Units](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/1-3-the-language-of-physics-physical-quantities-and-units):
  an alternative explanation of units and conversions. The “Check Your Understanding”
  questions 12–14 provide multiple-choice practice on metric units and conversions;
  some use US customary units, so they are supplementary practice rather than a Cambridge topic checklist.
- [§5.1 Vector Addition and Subtraction: Graphical Methods](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/5-1-vector-addition-and-subtraction-graphical-methods):
  head-to-tail drawings, resultant vectors, subtraction and a perpendicular-force example.
  Start with the two-vector examples; the longer three-vector walk is optional extension.
- [§5.2 Analytical Methods](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/5-2-vector-addition-and-subtraction-analytical-methods):
  optional further study using components and trigonometry. A full treatment goes beyond
  the IGCSE requirement for two perpendicular forces or velocities.

## 3. Visual practice

[PhET Vector Addition — launch the simulation](https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/vector-addition/latest/vector-addition_en.html)
lets him change arrow lengths and directions and display their sum.

Suggested task: predict the resultant, then check it in the simulation, for two arrows
in the same direction, two in opposite directions, and two at right angles. For the
last case, try magnitudes 3 and 4: the resultant magnitude is 5. Ask him to explain
why ordinary addition gives the wrong magnitude in that last case.

## 4. Cambridge examination practice

[Official IGCSE Physics past papers and specimen papers](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625/past-papers/)
provide examination questions and mark schemes. Paper 1 is Core multiple choice;
Paper 2 is Extended multiple choice. For the formal vector topic, use Paper 2.
These papers cover the whole course, so select relevant questions rather than assigning
a complete paper at this stage. The public collection is a selection of the available papers.

If a Cambridge-specific coursebook is wanted, the
[Cambridge IGCSE Physics Coursebook with Digital Access](https://www.cambridgebookshop.co.uk/products/cambridge-igcse-physics-coursebook-with-digital-access-2-years),
ISBN **9781108888073**, is a paid option. The free readings above are enough to begin
these two topics without purchasing a book.

## Suggested three-session sequence

1. **Units, 30–40 minutes:** read the selected §1.3 pages, then attempt Exercise 1-1.
   Check mistakes by writing the conversion factor explicitly, with units.
2. **Vectors, 30–40 minutes:** read definitions and diagrams, work through the PhET
   task, then use OpenStax §5.1 for resultant-vector practice.
3. **Multiple choice, 20–30 minutes:** complete Siyavula's four vector MCQs and
   OpenStax's selected units questions. For each answer, explain the reasoning before
   checking the key. Revisit errors after a day or two.

If his course is actually AS & A Level Physics 9702, these remain useful foundations,
but add the AS requirements for base/derived units, checking equation homogeneity,
and resolving vectors into perpendicular components; use 9702 Paper 1 for MCQ practice.
